Deployment timeline

October 2026 to December 2029 · scroll sideways on small screens

Campaign timing follows what the law requires each group to pay, not what has been announced in principle. Diesel, EV and PHEV owners pay RUC today. Petrol drivers do not, and no start date has been set.

Planned campaign Conditional on a decision not yet made Legislative or product milestone

Legislation. The Land Transport (Revenue) Amendment Bill, which enables digital licences and private RUC providers, had not passed when Parliament dissolved on 1 October. On 31 August the Transport Minister said it was unlikely to pass before the 7 November election and that moving petrol vehicles onto RUC could start being looked at around 2028–29 if the Government is re-elected. No party has set a date. A change of government could move this in either direction.

Price triggers. The 12c fuel excise rise planned for 1 January 2027, and the equivalent RUC rise, were cancelled on 31 August. The next scheduled increase is 1 January 2028. That is the next date on which current RUC payers will notice a price change, so it is the natural re-engagement moment.

Product. The press release draft has ROADRUNR with early users in November 2026 and broadly available from mid-December. The model’s launch window therefore starts mid-December, over summer holiday driving, when unexpected long trips are the second most common cause of running over a RUC licence.

What changed from the original plan. The original recommendation held about 31% of budget as a gated tranche for petrol and family personas, released if a petrol RUC date was legislated by 30 June 2027. On current information that release will not happen inside this programme, so the tranche moves to diesel, EV and PHEV owners.

Persona inputs

Take rate: Van Westendorp acceptance at $3/month (Pretian). App preference: share choosing either eRUC option over “no app”, used for pay-per-purchase. Overage: expected purchases per year above the 3 included. Purchases: total per year, used for pay-per-purchase revenue.

Open questions for EROAD

Answers to these would move the model more than any media change
  • Which pricing model launches in December? Pretian’s update says EROAD believes pay-per-purchase suits launch; Pretian suggests the $3/month subscription. At pay-per-purchase, paid media needs a CPI near US$2.60 to pay back, so the media plan would lean much harder on search capture and owned channels.
  • Can ROADRUNR be shown inside the RUC transactions EROAD already processes? The press release says EROAD processes around 83% of New Zealand’s RUC transactions on behalf of the Crown. If any of that flow can carry a ROADRUNR prompt, owned installs would rise well above the 25–30% share assumed here, and that is the largest single lever on portfolio ROAS.
  • Is the 3-purchase cap per account or per vehicle? This decides whether multi-vehicle households generate overage revenue or simply more subscriptions.
  • Early-user data from November. Install-to-paid conversion, purchases per user and vehicle mix from the early cohort should replace the realisation, overage and take-rate assumptions before the mid-December budget is committed.
  • Purchase frequency. Pretian’s pay-per-purchase revenue of $12.50 a year implies about 2.5 purchases a year. The Kantar survey’s current payers imply about 3.9. EROAD’s transaction data can settle this.
  • Is an Auto or CaaS tier planned for 2027? PHEV drivers showed the highest acceptance of the automatic tier (38%), so an upgrade test belongs on the timeline if the tier exists.

Method. Per persona: paying subscribers = installs × min(1, take rate × adjustment × realisation). 12-month revenue = $3 × 12 + expected overage purchases × $7 (subscription) or $5 × purchases (pay-per-purchase). 3-year value = 12-month revenue × (1 + r + r²) with retention r. ROAS = 3-year value × gross margin ÷ cost per paying subscriber. Installs saturate towards 12% of each pool. Portfolio figures add owned and earned installs at each persona’s assumed share. Breakeven CPI scales the Base CPI by ROAS ÷ hurdle.
